# Echoes of the Maya — 360° Projection Mapping

A permanent installation at Mexico City's National Museum of Anthropology, transforming the central courtyard into a living canvas for pre-Hispanic mythology. The experience runs nightly for 22 minutes and has been viewed by over 800,000 visitors since opening.

## The Brief

The museum wanted to breathe new life into its existing collection of Maya stelae without removing or modifying any artifacts. The challenge: create an experience that feels timeless, reverent, and authentically rooted in the source material — not a generic "cool projection mapping" demo.

## The Solution

We 3D-scanned the courtyard at sub-millimeter resolution using photogrammetry (4,200 reference photos) and LiDAR fusion, building a master UV unwrap that accounted for every irregularity in the limestone walls. The content was hand-painted by a team of 6 Mexican illustrators working in close consultation with the museum's curators of Mesoamerican studies, ensuring iconographic accuracy across 1,400+ individual symbols.

Eight 4K laser projectors, edge-blended and color-calibrated, wrap the courtyard in 360 degrees of generative animation. The audio-reactive layer was built in MaxMSP, driving visual parameters from a custom composition that combines period instruments (ocarinas, conch shells, teponaztli drums) with spatialized ambient textures.

## The Impact

- Permanent installation viewed by 800,000+ visitors in first 18 months
- Average visit time increased 3.4x for courtyard-adjacent galleries
- Featured in UNESCO's 2024 report on technology in cultural heritage
- Project lead Maria Gonzalez invited to speak at SXSW 2025
- Won Bronze at the 2025 AAM MUSE Awards
